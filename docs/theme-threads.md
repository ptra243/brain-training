# Theme threads, for review

Summaries of the story threads in `src/content/talk/`. Write under any bullet, or on the Notes line at the end of a card. Greetings, borrowed lines, face lines and the opening are one-liners, so they are not here. Pair threads and victim threads are still empty.

The threads share one voice: dinner-table hearsay, a dry last line, nobody lying and nobody naming a card. Each card keeps its own joke.

## Direction, not settled

A card shared by several guests made every plot "somebody once". The specific story is the pair: this guest on this card. Financier + Emperor is the man who wants the books done properly in a house that would not survive that. The Host's Emperor is the household rules, and the Butler's is not the family's accounts.

What switches is which of a guest's cards was rolled (home, or one of two borrows, upright or reversed). That picks which of their own motives she hears. During the case the motive is why the room feels about him as it does, and why talk points at him. For the Financier on the Emperor: everyone dislikes him because he would open the books, even when he is alive, and if the victim had put the house in disrepute that dislike reads as guilt. None of it is a clue. The ending can still say he killed or was killed for it.

10 guests × 3 cards × 2 readings = 60 motives. The outlines go in `docs/motive-outlines.xlsx` before any lines are rewritten. One row is filled in: the Financier on the Emperor, upright. `docs/secrets.md` is the same job; leave its six kinds until these pairs are enough.

## To decide

These four rope stories go darker than the rest.

## Emperor

Orders, the house run properly. Reversed is cold control.

- A household rulebook was drawn up and nobody read past the first page. Order is whoever holds the pen.
- A decision nobody was allowed to question turned out to be the right one, which only made it worse. People still whisper it at dinner.
- A senseless order was followed to the letter, purely to make a point. Nobody has dared give one like it since.
- A ledger of favours is kept by someone who is not the person owed. It has footnotes. Better not to be in it, and worse to be left out.
- Knife, upright: it carved the first joint, and only the head of the house was meant to use it. It has stayed in its case.
- Knife, reversed: a poor gift. The giver wanted something back, so it was never used, out of spite.
- Candlestick, upright: it stood at the head of every great dinner, lit first and snuffed last. The house has run on that rule.
- Candlestick, reversed: a gift from someone the house would rather forget, put away whenever the head of the house was in a temper. It has been put away a lot.
- Vase, upright: the first thing bought for the house, a whole wing planned around it, dusted on a strict schedule.
- Vase, reversed: bought to impress a visitor who never came back. Nobody may admit it is ugly, so it is admired very loudly, by order.

## Empress

Feeding people, fussing. Reversed is managing everyone for their own good.

- A family recipe was never written down. A cousin copied it wrong on purpose. The real one is still safe.
- Someone cried over a plate of leftovers and nobody asked why. A kind word would have fixed it. It stuck.
- Someone was told they looked tired, three times in one evening. It was a hint, not a kindness. They have not worn that colour since.
- A family portrait was moved because someone disliked the angle. It was a very pointed gift. Nobody mentions it now.
- Rope, upright: it held a children's swing, a child tied their first knot with it, and nobody could throw it out.
- Rope, reversed: left coiled when the nursery closed. A child was punished with it once. Nobody speaks of the swing now.
- Wrench, upright: it mended the nursery radiator one hard winter, then was wrapped in a tea towel like something precious.
- Wrench, reversed: taken to the boiler in a temper. The boiler never forgave the house, and the hot water still sulks on bath nights.
- Vase, upright: flowers for every christening, never allowed to stand empty, even in the hardest winter.
- Vase, reversed: a peace offering after a wedding quarrel, filled with the couple's least favourite flower on every anniversary since.

## Hierophant

Tradition, manners, things written down. Reversed is rules bent correctly.

- A visiting relative broke a custom by accident. Nobody said a word, which was the punishment, and there was no second invitation.
- An old yearly toast has a second verse nobody remembers. Everyone mumbles through it.
- A rule was invented on the spot to settle an argument, then written up to look old, and has been quoted solemnly ever since.
- The list of exceptions is longer than the list of rules, and the exceptions all belong to the same few people.
- Knife, upright: carried in a feast-day procession and blessed with the bread. The family still lowers its voice about it.
- Knife, reversed: "blessed" three times by three different men, the blessings sold by the slice. Nobody has asked for a refund.
- Candlestick, upright: blessed by a visiting bishop, a vow made over it. Break the vow and you will not sleep easy.
- Candlestick, reversed: never blessed. Someone paid for the story, and the bishop was no bishop.
- Poison, upright: an old rat remedy, the label copied in the same hand for three generations, kept on the highest shelf by custom.
- Poison, reversed: sold to the family as a tonic with a solemn blessing. Calling the recipe sacred kept anyone from reading it.

## Magician

Patter, tricks, an audience. Reversed is a trick that gets you into trouble.

- A coin trick once made a sulky child laugh. The child grew up serious and still asks for the coin back.
- A chosen card turned up baked inside a pie. The cook was furious, the guests delighted, and nobody wants the method explained.
- A trick went wrong in front of an important audience. They applauded anyway, which was worse. The patter afterwards was magnificent.
- A wager was made on a trick that had not been invented yet. It was invented on the spot, very nearly worked, and the loser still wants a rematch.
- Candlestick, upright: it vanished and turned up where it was. A guest borrowed it for a trick and gave it back warm. It seems eager to return.
- Candlestick, reversed: it has sold itself twice, to two families. Lost in a card game and won back before the silver was counted. The one on show is not the real one.
- Rope, upright: cut in half at a dinner and restored. Taught by a nameless travelling performer. Nobody has worked out the knot.
- Rope, reversed: meant for an escape act. The knots held better than anyone intended, the act was retired, and the rope was kept.
- Poison, upright: it played itself in a stage act, swapped for coloured water, and the audience never knew which bottle they were watching.
- Poison, reversed: sold as a cure for bad luck. The buyer's luck did not improve, there was no refund, and the seller is still at it.

## High Priestess

Noticing, keeping secrets. Reversed is enjoying that you know she knows.

- Someone confided something very quietly at dinner. Three people pretended not to hear, and none of them has mentioned it since.
- A family secret everyone knows and nobody says is really quite small, which is why it has lasted.
- A secret changed hands for a very small favour, never repaid, on purpose. Whoever holds it now is enjoying it.
- Somebody keeps a neat file on everyone else. Nobody has seen it, which is how it stays effective.
- Wrench, upright: it lives in the one drawer nobody dusts. Some doors only open to it.
- Wrench, reversed: nobody asks who last oiled it. It opened a door that should have stayed locked. The key it replaced is still somewhere in the house.
- Poison, upright: kept under lock longer than anyone remembers. Only one person knew what it was for, and they never told.
- Poison, reversed: never labelled, on purpose. Someone mistook it for cordial until they sniffed the cork. Nobody will say whose nose.
- Vase, upright: said to hum when the house is about to have news. A fortune-teller read the cracks and never repeated the reading.
- Vase, reversed: a crack nobody will admit to. Glued back in secret, and the mender has kept quiet ever since.

## Devil

Favours, debts, a smile that is the contract. Reversed is a bargain that has come due.

- Someone was lent a great deal, with a smile and no paperwork. The smile was the paperwork. They have been very polite ever since.
- A dinner bet was for something other than money. Both sides thought they had won. Neither has got over it.
- An old bargain is about to come due, and nobody will say whose. The terms were never written down.
- A favour was called in recently, and not gently. The answer was a very long pause.
- Knife, upright: part of a bargain, changed hands for a favour nobody could name. The family calls it a fair price.
- Knife, reversed: it came with strings. A debt that was not quite a debt was settled with it, and people who are offered it tend to owe someone afterwards.
- Rope, upright: a promise made to hold. A deal sealed with one end each. People remember a deal like that.
- Rope, reversed: used to hold more than boxes. It kept someone to a bad bargain, and they still will not look at it.
- Poison, upright: came into the family as part of a deal. The other side wanted the label more than the bottle. Nobody knows who won.
- Poison, reversed: promised to three different people, each told it was a secret. It is the only promise nobody has tried to collect.

## Hermit

Quiet, a closed door. Reversed is sulking because nobody noticed the sulk.

- Someone walked out of a family gathering and nobody noticed until the pudding. That was the point. It was the most peaceful pudding anyone remembers.
- There is a chair nobody else sits in. Nobody remembers why. It is the best chair.
- A letter went unanswered for a very long time. The reply was written, kept, and rewritten, and never sent, out of principle.
- Someone stormed out of a gathering years ago and left their coat. The coat has been brushed every year since. Nobody will say if that is spite or hope.
- Candlestick, upright: it belonged to someone who liked to read alone, and who burnt through a hundred candles before sharing one. The house has been dimmer since.
- Candlestick, reversed: nobody likes to say who it was left burning for. A letter was read by it and never answered. The wax is still there.
- Wrench, upright: left behind by someone who did not stay, who preferred fixing things to talking. The house is quieter for it.
- Wrench, reversed: nobody asked who left it. There was a quarrel and a slammed door. It has sat untouched.
- Vase, upright: given to someone who never had visitors. They kept one flower in it and talked to the flower. Best conversation in the house.
- Vase, reversed: sent back still in its paper, with a note that said only "No, thank you." Passed along ever since, from one recluse to the next.

## Chariot

Getting there first. Reversed is refusing to wait for a turn.

- Someone drew up a plan for the whole family, uninvited and colour-coded. It now holds a door open.
- Someone organised a family wedding down to the last sandwich and handed the couple a timetable. They were married exactly on schedule.
- A promise was made long ago about who would be in charge, and it was said in front of the wrong person. It has been repeated, with improvements, ever since.
- Someone rearranged the family portraits in order of importance, with their own at the front. The portraits went back. Nobody has forgotten.
- Knife, upright: a prize, won in a race nobody else knew they were running, polished ever since.
- Knife, reversed: it has cut a few corners. It once opened a letter before it was anyone's turn, and the letter was worth the hurry.
- Candlestick, upright: meant to light the way for the next in line, handed down with a short speech about duty. The speech grows longer each time.
- Candlestick, reversed: nobody waits for it to be lit properly. It was once marched up and down the stairs to hurry a party along. The wax went everywhere. The party did not hurry.
- Wrench, upright: it kept a very old hoist running, and nobody else was allowed near the works. It still goes beautifully, mostly in one direction.
- Wrench, reversed: it made that hoist faster than it was built to be. It went beautifully until the trays arrived first. The cook was less impressed.

## Sun

Making the room laugh. Reversed is a sulk when told no.

- Someone made a whole room laugh at a funeral, very gently. Nobody minded. It was the kindest thing anyone did that day.
- Someone got the whole family playing charades, even the stern ones, who were rather good. Nobody will say what they were acting.
- A birthday present was returned because it was the wrong shade. The giver was made to apologise. The shade has been named, in private, ever since.
- Someone sulked so hard that a family party was called off. The sulk lasted longer than the party would have.
- Rope, upright: it started as a skipping rope. A whole nursery learned to count on it, and nobody got past twenty without giggling.
- Rope, reversed: used to tie a door shut during a tantrum. The tantrum won. The door still sticks, out of loyalty.
- Candlestick, upright: it has stood on every birthday table. A cake was once balanced on it for a dare. The cake survived. The tablecloth did not.
- Candlestick, reversed: sulked at for a whole party, for being the wrong height for the photographs. It was moved, and the sulk followed it.
- Wrench, upright: a present for a child who wanted to build a rocket. The rocket was mostly a biscuit tin. It went nowhere, and everyone clapped.
- Wrench, reversed: taken to a music box for playing the wrong tune. The box has played nothing but the wrong tune since.

## Justice

Weighing things twice. Reversed is deciding first and fetching the facts after.

- A case was solved from a single dropped glove, which belonged to the person who solved it. It was returned with a very short note.
- Someone turned down a reward because a case was not closed to their liking, reopened it unpaid, and got the same answer, better documented.
- A famous case was decided before anyone had arrived. The facts were fetched afterwards, and they fitted beautifully.
- An innocent party was accused very confidently. The apology was a good deal less confident, and it came by post.
- Knife, upright: weighed in every kind of argument. A cook demanded a hearing over it. Settled fairly, which satisfied no one.
- Knife, reversed: blamed for a lot, and never given a fair hearing. It is easier to blame the knife.
- Poison, upright: evidence in a very long trial. The jury asked to see the bottle three times. It was exactly what the label said, which disappointed everyone.
- Poison, reversed: blamed for an upset stomach that turned out to be the oysters. Nobody apologised to the bottle.
- Wrench, upright: it settled a leaking-pipe quarrel. Each neighbour got exactly half the bill. The pipe, at least, was satisfied.
- Wrench, reversed: found guilty of breaking a boiler it never touched. The plumber got off with a warning. It has not been allowed near a boiler since.

## Lovers

A match, a fuss, a happy ending. Reversed is a choice put off.

- A match was arranged between two people who then refused to speak. They married other people, separately, out of spite. The matchmaker takes full credit.
- A wedding toast went on so long the cake collapsed. The couple were quietly grateful. It is still the best-attended wedding anyone remembers.
- An engagement was called off over the choice of hymns. Nobody has said which hymns. The ring is still in a drawer.
- A letter was written, torn up, and written again. The third version was the worst. It is probably still being carried about.
- Wrench, upright: two people met over a dripping tap and have never agreed on anything since. They are still very fond of each other.
- Wrench, reversed: left on a windowsill in a huff, thrown and missed. The tap is still dripping, and so is the feeling.
- Vase, upright: the only wedding present both sides agreed on, nearly returned twice, and it has outlasted the toaster and two sets of curtains.
- Vase, reversed: a love token sent back without a note, posted back and forth all summer. In the end neither would claim it, so the house kept it.
- Rope, upright: it tied the knot at a wedding, for a photograph. The couple could not get it undone and went to the reception attached.
- Rope, reversed: a parlour tug-of-war between two suitors. The prize left with a third party while they pulled. Both still claim they were winning.

## Star

Hope, being looked at kindly. Reversed is sparkling as a job.

- Someone made a wish out loud and was told off for it. It came true anyway, in a roundabout way. They are careful with wishes now.
- A performance ended in a standing ovation for the wrong person. The right person was gracious. Their smile has been brighter since.
- A bright young thing was told to tone it down, did, and was noticed at once. It was the dullest evening anyone remembers.
- A toast was prepared and then not given. It was very good. It is probably still in a pocket.
- Wrench, upright: a famous performer fixed a dressing-room tap with it, to prove a point. A theatre company borrowed it as a prop. It got better reviews than the play.
- Wrench, reversed: a prop in a play nobody liked. It got the only good notice, and has been resting ever since.
- Vase, upright: filled with flowers from an unsigned admirer every week for a year. It has looked hopeful ever since.
- Vase, reversed: knocked over on opening night, glued so carefully nobody can find the crack. It still flinches at applause.
- Poison, upright: the prettiest thing in the cupboard. Someone mistook it for perfume and praised the scent at length.
- Poison, reversed: bought for the mice, who are doing splendidly. The label was written in a hopeful hand. The bottle has never been opened.

## Death

A tidy ending. Reversed is refusing to let the ending happen.

- Someone cleared out a whole wardrobe in an afternoon, felt lighter and slightly cold, and calls it the best decision they ever regretted.
- A very old habit was given up one morning, without ceremony. Nobody noticed for a week. That was the part that stung.
- Someone kept every birthday card they were ever sent, a labelled box for each year. Nobody has dared suggest a tidy-up.
- A farewell was held twice, because the first one did not take. The second was even more awkward. The guest of honour is still around.
- Rope, upright: it once carried a coffin down the stairs, very gently, and was kept out of respect. Nobody would tie a parcel with it.
- Rope, reversed: it has seen things it will not talk about, and was used on the last day of something. The house has been waiting for it to be thrown out.
- Poison, upright: older than anyone now living in the house, bought for rats in a hard winter. The rats left of their own accord. The bottle stayed.
- Poison, reversed: meant to be thrown out at the end of an era, put on a high shelf instead, just for now. Just for now has lasted a long time.
- Knife, upright: it carved the last roast of a long tradition. The cook wept into the gravy and nobody minded. It has stayed in its box out of respect.
- Knife, reversed: meant to be retired, given a farewell with a speech, and back in the drawer the following week.

## Hanged Man

Letting go, waiting. Reversed is stuck, and still mentioning the sacrifice.

- Someone gave up a long-held argument halfway through dinner. Everyone was too surprised to pick it up. The soup was better that night.
- A very stubborn person once agreed to wait, for no reason anyone could name, and was the calmest they have ever been.
- Someone has had a long run of bad luck at cards. The relatives have quietly stopped paying the debts. The will has been redrafted, or near enough.
- Someone gave up a great deal and has been mentioning it ever since. Nobody asked them to. The sacrifice was mostly a hat.
- Rope, upright: bought from a failed enterprise at a very good price. Its former owner lost everything but the rope. Best investment the house ever made.
- Rope, reversed: bought in a hurry from someone who had lost everything. The sale was not entirely the owner's idea. Nobody asks what else came with it.
- Knife, upright: handed over to settle a debt, and worth more than the debt. The lender was too proud to say so. Both sides came away feeling generous.
- Knife, reversed: bought for a grand carving that never happened. The dinner was called off at the last minute. The knife is still waiting for its moment.
- Vase, upright: kept upside down for years as a doorstop, until a visitor read the mark and nearly fainted at the price. Right way up ever since, and smug about it.
- Vase, reversed: sold to pay a bill, bought back at twice the price. The dealer still laughs. Nobody in the house does.

## Moon

A memory that might be a draught. Reversed is a story told until it changes.

- Someone swore they saw a strange light at the window as a child. Nobody else ever did. They still check, every year.
- An old portrait's eyes were once seen to follow a guest. The guest moved the portrait instead. It has been slightly crooked ever since.
- A family story has been told so often that it has changed twice. The original is nothing like the version people prefer. Nobody wants to be the one to check.
- A sleepwalker wrote a long letter and posted it before waking. The reply was very confused. They have been writing to each other happily ever since.
- Candlestick, upright: said to burn bluer when rain is coming. A guest saw a face in the flame once, and put it down to the wine.
- Candlestick, reversed: it leaves shadows where none belong. A guest swore it moved and was laughed at. Nobody laughed the second time.
- Poison, upright: people insist it glows a little in the dark. A child once mistook it for a nightlight. It went up a shelf the next morning, and the story grew.
- Poison, reversed: an old label nobody can quite read. Three people have guessed what it says, each of them sure, and none of them agreed.
- Knife, upright: said to reflect a face that is not in the room. A guest polished it twice to be sure, and took up a different hobby afterwards.
- Knife, reversed: missing for a year, then back in the wrong drawer. Everyone swore they had already looked there. Nobody trusts that drawer now.
