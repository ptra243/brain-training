# The story: why everyone is at the house

Decided with the owner 2026-10-29. This is the frame every talk line, motive and opening hangs off.
Voice rules (plain modern English, nothing that needs time to have passed) are in `docs/theme-draft.md`.

## The evening

The family's chocolate company turns 50. The Host invites family, staff and a few others to the
family house for the weekend to celebrate. At breakfast the Host will announce whether the company
goes to one of the heirs or is sold to the Investor, whose fund has made an offer. Nobody knows which.

The Investor is one of the 10 guests (the Conjurer's slot and Magician card: the pitch and the charm),
so they can be a suspect or the victim. If the Investor dies, the offer dies with them.

The Fixer (the Butler's slot and Hierophant card: knows how things are done here, and keeps quiet
about it) is the third way it could go: the Host might hand the company to them instead of the family.
So the decision has four outcomes, each with people for and against it: the elder heir, the younger
heir, the Fixer, or a sale to the Investor.

A storm cuts the road, so nobody comes or goes. After dinner someone is killed. One of the guests did it.

It works whoever dies. If the Host is the victim, the decision never gets made, and that hangs over
everyone. In a campaign the later bodies keep raising the same question: who gains now?

## The people (draft for the owner)

Who each guest is, before working out how they fit together (`docs/relationships.md` predates this
cast and gets redone from here). Cards are listed because they decide which version of the guest she
meets: home card, then the two borrowed ones; upright is the lighter side, reversed the darker.
Gender (decided 2026-10-29): the Elder heir is the daughter and the Younger heir the son; the Host,
Hostess and Uncle are gendered by their titles, and the Fixer is a man. Everyone else is never given a gender. Lines shared
by a card (neutral lines, card threads) never use he or she, because they must fit every guest who rolls
that card. A guest's own lines (motive threads, bound and borrowed lines, bio) may.

**The heirs and the old prejudice.** She is the better at the job and has been "nearly ready" for
years; he left and wants none of it, yet half the table quietly assumes the son will come back and take
over. It stays in the background: a backhanded remark in someone's own lines ("In my day the eldest son
took over. Times change, I'm told."), never the narrator, and the game never agrees with it. For her it
is a grievance and a motive.

## How they fit together (draft for the owner)

Any guest can be the victim or the killer, and any seven are seated, so the backstory has two layers:

- **Fixed history**: true in every case, whoever is seated or dead. Nobody's version ever contradicts it.
- **Rolled secrets**: each guest's six versions (3 cards x upright/reversed) are six answers to the
  questions below. Only the version she hears is true in that case. Two versions that can be heard in
  the same case must not contradict each other. **Every version answers "why might they have done it?"**
  It must be one of the reasons people kill in a whodunnit: **money** (a lot of it), **exposure** (prison,
  ruin), **protecting someone**, **revenge** for a real wrong, or **freedom** from something unbearable.
  A grievance or gossip is not enough. The killer is drawn separately, so any version may turn out guilty.

### Fixed history
- **Fifty years ago** the Host and the Uncle opened one small shop. The bar that made it famous was
  the Hostess's recipe. The family all know this. The box has only the Host's name on it.
- **The Housekeeper** came to run the family house before either child was born.
- **The Fixer** started on the factory floor at seventeen. The Host noticed them, and they have been
  the Host's right hand ever since. The children grew up with them around.
- **Twenty years ago** the Uncle was pushed out after a fight nobody will explain. The Uncle kept
  their shares. The Housekeeper is the only one who stayed in touch.
- **Years ago the Detective** investigated the company. The case was dropped. The Detective, now retired,
  is the Hostess's old friend; the Hostess invited them to the 50th over the Host's objection.
- **The Lawyer** has been the family's lawyer for twenty years, from outside the family.
- **The Elder heir** joined the company straight from university and has been told it will be hers.
  **The Younger heir**, her brother, left after a row with the Host and built a life in the city.
- **For fifty years** every bar has been made with hazelnuts from one family orchard, a couple of
  hours' drive away. The Fixer goes out for every harvest. Cheaper nuts would change the taste.
- **Twenty years ago** the old factory burned down, and the insurance paid for the new one. How it
  started differs by version; no version says it was an accident or that it wasn't.
- **This spring** the Investor's fund made an offer. The Host called the weekend to give the answer.

### Three questions everyone has an answer to
1. **Whose company is it, really?** The Hostess's recipe, the Uncle's shares, the Fixer's work, the
   heirs' birthright, the Host's name on the box.
2. **Is it as healthy as it looks?** The Lawyer's two wills and the family trust, the Investor's offer, whatever the
   Fixer has made go away, why the Hostess invited the detective who once investigated it.
3. **Who will the Host pick?** Elder heir, Younger heir, the Fixer, or a sale. The Uncle's vote and the
   Hostess's opinion both count.

Every guest has a stake in at least two, so any seven at the table have plenty to argue about.

### Versions that touch each other (checked 2026-10-29)
Any two guests' versions can be heard in the same case, so these were checked to fit together:
- **The Uncle's fight** has three partial reasons: the recipe (Uncle, Death upright), taking the blame
  for the Host (Uncle, Hanged Man upright) and the Hostess (Hostess, Lovers upright). Each says
  "partly", so any two can be true at once.
- **Someone in the family talking to the Investor** (Investor, Devil upright) is unnamed, so it fits the
  Elder heir's Devil upright, the Uncle's promised shares, or nobody else's version at all.
- **The company in trouble** (Host, Hanged Man reversed) fits the Lawyer's family-trust loss and the
  Investor's struggling fund; none of them says the company is fine.
- **The dropped case** (Detective) fits the Fixer making it go away and the Detective letting something
  slide for the Hostess: both can be part of why it was dropped.
- **Who the Host picks**: the sealed decision (Host), the son asked to take over (Younger heir) and the
  Fixer's hopes are all rumours of a decision nobody has seen, so they never cancel out.
- **The Host's affair** (Hostess, Star reversed) never names the other person. The Investor's Devil
  reversed ("met someone here before, under another name … more than business") can be the other half;
  when both come up, she can connect them herself. Neither version says it outright.
- **The fire** (Fixer, High Priestess upright) was the same year the Uncle was pushed out. The Uncle's
  Hanged Man upright ("took the blame for something the Host did") can be read as the fire; neither says so.
  "I was told the place would be empty" hints someone was inside. Kept on purpose (owner, 2026-10-29).
- **The pollution** (Fixer, High Priestess reversed) would sink the sale and the inheritance, so it
  gives several guests a reason to want it buried, as background only.
- **Watch when writing lines**: the Housekeeper knowing "the Younger heir's secret" must stay vague, since
  his secret differs by version.

## How motives use it

Every motive answers one question: what does this guest gain or lose depending on the company, and
the decision? The tarot card picks which version she hears (home or borrowed card, upright or
reversed). Chocolate gives the jokes: the secret recipe, tasting, the rival brand, the buyer.

Still true from `docs/theme-draft.md`: a motive is never a clue, never says where anyone was or what
they held, and never depends on who the killer is. Nobody lies.

**How a thread reads (decided 2026-10-29): guarded questioning, three beats.** Nobody confesses to a
stranger, and later beats only open as she finishes games about the guest, so a thread is built like a
real questioning:
1. Her opening line, then **the public story**: what they would tell anyone. Nothing incriminating.
2. **She brings something she learned.** Vary the source across a guest's six and across guests:
   something she found, overheard, was told ("someone said…", never naming the guest), or noticed.
   A stranger finding five documents in one evening strains belief. They half-admit, defensively.
3. **The death and what it changes for them.** Beat 3 is about what this guest gains or loses now that
   someone has died, never about what the victim did to them. It must hold for any victim and in any
   campaign round, because a guest's thread stays the same while `{victim}` changes. The shared lever:
   any death this weekend puts the sale and the announcement on hold. The motive shows as how it looks,
   never as a confession; she works out the rest.

Vary the motive kinds across a guest's six (exposure, money, protecting someone, revenge, freedom), so
"the victim was about to expose me" is not everyone's story. A thread may break the shape (openly hostile
from beat 1, or volunteering the motive because they think it clears them).

Each beat is 2 to 4 sentences, enough to state the key facts plainly; each of her lines is written for
that thread and becomes the button. Her lines say what she found, never where or when tonight. The test:
after reading, she can explain the motive in one sentence, and every line is something a real person
would say to a stranger at that point. Past dates and places are fine ("twelve years ago", "at the
factory"); tonight's whereabouts, times and objects are not. Beat 3 never mentions the boss separately
from `{victim}`, since the victim may be the Host.

**Nothing that hints at a weapon.** No nut allergies, and no chemicals, sprays or anything that
sounds like poison in a motive: poison is a weapon, so it would read as a clue to how someone died.
Pollution is factory waste, never a chemical.

### The Fixer

**Hierophant upright (the orchard)**
- **You:** You've been here longer than most of the family.
- **Fixer:** Thirty years. I started on the wrapping line at seventeen. These days I look after the suppliers, which mostly means one hazelnut orchard. Every bar we've ever made has their nuts in it.
- **You:** I overheard someone say the company pays your wife's family double for those nuts.
- **Fixer:** Whoever said it is right. I met my wife at my first harvest, and I've gone back every autumn since. We pay well because their nuts are why the bar tastes the way it does. The buyer's people have already said they'll switch to cheaper ones.
- **You:** And now {victim} is dead.
- **Fixer:** {victim} is dead, and nobody's selling anything this weekend. Every month the sale waits is another harvest for my wife's family. I know exactly how that sounds.

**Hierophant reversed (makes problems go away)**
- **You:** Everyone says you're the one who makes problems go away.
- **Fixer:** That's a nice way of putting it. Someone has a problem, they bring it to me, and after a while they don't have it any more. It's mostly paperwork. Most things are.
- **You:** Someone told me the police looked into the company's books once, and the case vanished in a week.
- **Fixer:** It didn't vanish. The boss rang me the night it started, and I spent a week making sure there was nothing to find. There wasn't. Nobody has asked me how since. That's the job.
- **You:** {victim} is dead. Was that one of your problems?
- **Fixer:** If {victim} had become a problem for this family, everyone at this table knows who'd have been asked to deal with it. I'm aware of that. So is everyone else.

**Emperor upright (the top job)**
- **You:** Who actually runs this company?
- **Fixer:** The boss's name is on the box. I just make sure there's something in it. Suppliers, staff, the machines, the bank. If it breaks, I fix it.
- **You:** I noticed the boss checks with you before answering anything about the company.
- **Fixer:** Thirty years will do that. When hazelnut prices doubled, the plan that saved us was mine. The boss presented it and took the applause at the Christmas party. I clapped along. I'm good at clapping.
- **You:** {victim} is dead. Does that change who gets the top job?
- **Fixer:** Any death in this family moves the pieces around. Fewer voices at the table, and the boss with more on their mind than tradition. I'd be a fool not to see who that might help.

**Emperor reversed (impossible to replace)**
- **You:** What happens to this place without you?
- **Fixer:** Five years ago the boss tried to find out. Brought in a new manager, very clever, lovely suit. Lasted six weeks.
- **You:** Nobody else seems to know the supplier passwords, or how the safe opens. Only you.
- **Fixer:** Not on purpose. Nobody else ever bothered to learn. The boss's kids? One left, and the other has spent eight years being "nearly ready". Someone has to hold the keys.
- **You:** {victim} is dead. Does that change anything for you?
- **Fixer:** There was talk of replacing me once the sale went through. A death puts every plan on hold, that one included. Funny how things work out.

**High Priestess upright (the fire)**
- **You:** You don't seem surprised by much.
- **Fixer:** Twenty years here does that. I've seen this company nearly go under twice. The second time, the old factory burned down and the insurance paid for the new one. Lucky, everyone said.
- **You:** I've been going through the old files. Every form from the year of the fire has your signature on it. Only yours.
- **Fixer:** I was twenty-two, and proud to be trusted with it. The delivery notes, the night log, the insurance claim. Nobody asked me to sign anything else that year. I've only lately wondered why.
- **You:** {victim} is dead, and there's a sale on the table.
- **Fixer:** The buyer's lawyers were going to start on the old files next week. With a death in the house, nobody's buying anything for a while, and those files stay in their boxes. I can see what that looks like.

**High Priestess reversed (the pollution)**
- **You:** Does anyone here have a reason to be scared of you?
- **Fixer:** Scared is a strong word. Careful, maybe. I've been here a long time, and I read everything that crosses my desk. People forget that.
- **You:** Someone said you keep copies of everything. Including the factory's waste reports.
- **Fixer:** Then someone has been in my desk. Yes. The river below the factory has been dead for ten years. I never reported it. I mentioned once to the boss that I'd kept copies, and my job has been very safe since.
- **You:** {victim} is dead. What happens to your copies now?
- **Fixer:** There are two kinds of people at this table: the ones who'd pay to keep those reports quiet, and the ones who'd love them in a newspaper. {victim} was one or the other. Either way, my copies are worth more now.
