# The story: why everyone is at the house

Decided with the owner 2026-10-29. This is the frame every talk line, motive and opening hangs off.
Voice rules (plain modern English, nothing that needs time to have passed) are in `docs/theme-draft.md`.

## The evening

The family's chocolate company turns 50. The Host invites family, staff and a few others to the
family house for the weekend to celebrate. At breakfast the Host will announce whether the company
goes to one of the heirs or is sold to the Investor, whose fund has made an offer. Nobody knows which.

The Investor is one of the 10 guests (the Conjurer's slot and Magician card: the pitch and the charm),
so they can be a suspect or the victim. If the Investor dies, the offer dies with them.

The Fixer (the Butler's slot and Hierophant card: knows how things are done here, and keeps quiet
about it) is the third way it could go: the Host might hand the company to them instead of the family.
So the decision has four outcomes, each with people for and against it: the elder heir, the younger
heir, the Fixer, or a sale to the Investor.

A storm cuts the road, so nobody comes or goes. After dinner someone is killed. One of the guests did it.

It works whoever dies. If the Host is the victim, the decision never gets made, and that hangs over
everyone. In a campaign the later bodies keep raising the same question: who gains now?

## The people (draft for the owner)

Who each guest is, before working out how they fit together (`docs/relationships.md` predates this
cast and gets redone from here). Cards are listed because they decide which version of the guest she
meets: home card, then the two borrowed ones; upright is the lighter side, reversed the darker.
Guests are never given a gender in lines.

### The Host: Emperor (borrows Hierophant, Hanged Man)
Built the company from one shop into a brand everyone knows. Generous, loud, used to being right.
Runs the family like the company: decisions come down, not up. Has put off naming a successor for
years; this weekend they finally have to.
- Plots: why decide now (bad news from a doctor, the company in trouble, or just tired)? Has the
  decision already been made and written down? Is someone being set up to lose?

### The Hostess: Empress (borrows Star, Lovers)
Married to the Host for thirty years. Warm, funny, feeds everyone. The first recipe, the one that
made the company, was theirs; the Host's name went on the box. Has never said this out loud in public.
- Plots: wants the credit at last; wants the company sold so the family is free of it; an old flame
  (Lovers) who is closer to home than anyone thinks.

### The Fixer: Hierophant (borrows Emperor, High Priestess)
Started on the factory floor at seventeen and worked up to the person who sorts out every problem
the Host has. Calm, capable, knows how everything works. The family's kids grew up with them around
and never decided whether they're staff or family.
- Plots: the Host might hand them the company; they've covered up something big for the Host; they
  know a secret about every guest and have never used one.

### The Investor: Magician (borrows Star, Devil)
Runs the fund that made the offer. Charming, quick, very good at making people feel clever. Came in
person to close the deal and brought expensive wine. Nobody knows how much they actually know about
the company.
- Plots: the offer is a bluff to buy cheap and close the factory; they've been talking privately to
  one of the family; they met someone here before, under another name.

### The Housekeeper: High Priestess (borrows Hermit, Death)
Has run the family house for longer than the kids have been alive. Quiet, dry, misses nothing.
Treated as part of the furniture, which suits them. Sees what the family leaves lying around.
- Plots: knows who changed what in the Host's papers; promised something in a will years ago; has
  been quietly looking after the Uncle's interests all along.

### The Accountant: Devil (borrows Emperor, Hanged Man)
Keeps the company's books, and a few people's private ones. Precise, dry, a little too pleased to
know what everyone is worth. Lends money on the side to people who can't ask their family.
- Plots: the numbers don't add up and they know why; half the table owes them; they lost money on a
  sure thing and covered it with company money.

### The Uncle: Hermit (borrows Death, Hanged Man)
The Host's brother-in-arms from the first shop, and family by marriage. Pushed out of the company
twenty years ago after a fight nobody explains. Still owns shares, so still has a vote. Came back for
the first time in years and keeps to the edges of the room.
- Plots: wants revenge, or wants peace; the fight was about the recipe; their vote decides a sale.

### The Elder heir: Chariot (borrows Emperor, Devil)
Works at the company and has been told for years it will be theirs. Driven, impatient, good at the
job and knows it. Has already made plans, and maybe promises, on the strength of it.
- Plots: has borrowed against an inheritance they don't have yet; is quietly talking to the Investor
  in case it all goes wrong; thinks the Fixer is in the way.

### The Younger heir: Sun (borrows Lovers, Star)
Everyone's favourite. Left the company to do something of their own in the city and is happy there,
or says so. Easy-going, funny, the one people tell things to at parties.
- Plots: has secretly been asked to take over instead; is in love with someone the family would hate;
  needs money for their own business and the sale would give it.

### The Detective: Justice (borrows High Priestess, Hermit)
A retired police detective and the Host's oldest friend. Fair, stubborn, a little smug. Treats you as
a rival from the moment the body is found.
- Plots: the Host invited them because something is wrong at the company; they once let something
  slide for the Host and have regretted it since; they're sure who did it, and sometimes they're wrong.

## How they fit together (draft for the owner)

Any guest can be the victim or the killer, and any seven are seated, so the backstory has two layers:

- **Fixed history**: true in every case, whoever is seated or dead. Nobody's version ever contradicts it.
- **Rolled secrets**: each guest's six versions (3 cards x upright/reversed) are six answers to the
  questions below. Only the version she hears is true in that case. Two versions that can be heard in
  the same case must not contradict each other.

### Fixed history
- **Fifty years ago** the Host and the Uncle opened one small shop. The bar that made it famous was
  the Hostess's recipe. The family all know this. The box has only the Host's name on it.
- **The Housekeeper** came to run the family house before either child was born.
- **The Fixer** started on the factory floor at seventeen. The Host noticed them, and they have been
  the Host's right hand ever since. The children grew up with them around.
- **Twenty years ago** the Uncle was pushed out after a fight nobody will explain. The Uncle kept
  their shares. The Housekeeper is the only one who stayed in touch.
- **The Detective** and the Host have been friends since school. The Detective retired from the police
  last year.
- **The Accountant** was brought in by the Host ten years ago, from outside the family.
- **The Elder heir** joined the company straight from university and has been told it will be theirs.
  **The Younger heir** left after a row with the Host and built a life in the city.
- **This spring** the Investor's fund made an offer. The Host called the weekend to give the answer.

### Three questions everyone has an answer to
1. **Whose company is it, really?** The Hostess's recipe, the Uncle's shares, the Fixer's work, the
   heirs' birthright, the Host's name on the box.
2. **Is it as healthy as it looks?** The Accountant's numbers, the Investor's offer, whatever the
   Fixer has made go away, why the Host invited a retired detective.
3. **Who will the Host pick?** Elder heir, Younger heir, the Fixer, or a sale. The Uncle's vote and the
   Hostess's opinion both count.

Every guest has a stake in at least two, so any seven at the table have plenty to argue about.

### Who pulls which way
Fixed feelings, so talk and pair threads have something to lean on. Close (+), tension (−).

| Guest | Close to | Tension with |
|---|---|---|
| Host | Detective, Fixer | Uncle, Hostess (the credit), Elder heir (the promise) |
| Hostess | Housekeeper, Younger heir | Host (the credit); something old with the Uncle |
| Fixer | Host, Younger heir | Elder heir (the job), Accountant (both know the numbers) |
| Investor | (courts) Uncle, Hostess | Accountant (wants the real numbers), Fixer (in the way) |
| Housekeeper | Hostess, Uncle | Fixer (one knows the house, one the company) |
| Accountant | (nobody) | Fixer, Investor, Detective (asks too many questions) |
| Uncle | Housekeeper; something old with the Hostess | Host |
| Elder heir | Host (wants to be) | Fixer, Younger heir |
| Younger heir | Fixer, Hostess | Elder heir; Host (the row) |
| Detective | Host | Accountant; and you |

### Answers the versions can give (examples, not final)
- Why the Uncle was pushed out: the recipe (Uncle wanted the Hostess's name on the box); money
  (the Accountant's predecessor found something); the Hostess (the old flame).
- Why the Host invited the Detective: money is missing; an anonymous letter; just an old friend.
- Why the Younger heir left: refused to be the heir; the row was about the Fixer; the Host found out
  about someone they loved.
- What the Fixer made go away: a bad batch, a lawsuit, someone's debt.

## How motives use it

Every motive answers one question: what does this guest gain or lose depending on the company, and
the decision? The tarot card picks which version she hears (home or borrowed card, upright or
reversed). Chocolate gives the jokes: the secret recipe, tasting, the rival brand, the buyer.

Still true from `docs/theme-draft.md`: a motive is never a clue, never says where anyone was or what
they held, and never depends on who the killer is. Nobody lies.

## Reaction to the death (draft)

Shown under the greeting before she asks anything; warm or cold pool by the guest's current mood,
picked once per guest on its own seeded stream, avoiding lines another guest already has.

Warm:
- {victim} is gone, and the whole house has gone quiet.
- {victim} is dead. I've had two drinks and it still doesn't feel real.
- {victim} would have loved all this fuss. That's the worst part.
- {victim} is dead. I'm glad it's you asking and not one of the others.
- {victim} is dead. I keep forgetting, and then remembering.
- {victim} always joked this house would kill someone. Nobody's laughing now.
